This appendix supports the results in Chapter~\ref{ch:benchmark_results} by comparing topologies optimized with a laminar formulation against topologies optimized with the turbulent SA formulation. The comparison is performed for the pipe-bend and U-bend benchmarks from Bayat et al.~\cite{Bayat2026}.

\section{Methodology and Evaluation Pipeline}
\label{sec:app_C_lam_vs_turb_methodology}
Each benchmark is solved twice. The laminar reference design is optimized at $Re=1$, where the optimizer primarily responds to creeping-flow viscous losses. The turbulent design is optimized at the target high-Reynolds operating condition using the SA topology optimization framework described in Chapter~\ref{ch:topology_optimization_methodology}. Apart from replacing the SA turbulent state equations by the laminar flow formulation and setting $Re=1$, the laminar topology-optimization runs use the same benchmark-specific FEniCS setup as the turbulent Alexandersen runs: identical mesh, design-domain restrictions, non-design regions, volume constraint, uniform prescribed inlet velocity profile, pressure outlet, inlet-pressure objective, Brinkman penalty limits, filter radius, projection threshold, continuation schedules, MMA move limits, and maximum MMA iterations. This keeps the laminar-vs-turbulent comparison focused on the flow-physics model rather than on optimization or boundary-condition choices.

Because these FEniCS physical and numerical settings are inherited directly from the turbulent benchmark definitions in Appendix~\ref{app:B_validation_configurations}, they are not repeated as separate configuration tables in this appendix. The additional tables reported here are therefore limited to the Ansys mesh-independence studies for the extracted laminar geometries and the final CFD metric comparisons against the turbulent-design results from Chapter~\ref{ch:benchmark_results}.

After optimization, both projected density fields are thresholded and extracted as sharp geometries. The extracted laminar and turbulent designs are then evaluated in Ansys Fluent under the same turbulent operating condition. Because Chapter~\ref{ch:benchmark_results} already validates the extracted turbulent topologies with both Ansys-SA and Ansys-SST, the laminar-design comparison in this appendix is evaluated with the higher-fidelity SST $k$-$\omega$ closure. This keeps the comparison focused on topology performance under an independent two-equation turbulence model, rather than repeating the SA cross-code validation for the laminar reference geometries. The key performance metrics are viscous dissipation and pressure drop. The purpose of this appendix is not merely to show that the topologies look different, but to test whether the turbulent topology actually performs better when both designs are exposed to the same turbulent flow.

For fairness, the extracted laminar geometries receive their own Ansys mesh-independence checks. The turbulent-design grid studies are reported in Table~\ref{tab:app_B_alexandersen_pipebend_mesh} of Section~\ref{sec:app_B_config_alexandersen_pipebend} and Table~\ref{tab:app_B_alexandersen_ubend_mesh} of Section~\ref{sec:app_B_config_alexandersen_ubend} in Appendix~\ref{app:B_validation_configurations}; this appendix adds the corresponding laminar-design mesh tables and final laminar-vs-turbulent performance comparisons.

\section{Pipe-Bend Benchmark}
\label{sec:app_C_alexandersen_pipebend}

The pipe bend tests whether a topology optimized under turbulent conditions produces a more separation-aware $90^\circ$ turning path than a laminar topology optimized at $Re=1$. The turbulent case in Chapter~\ref{ch:benchmark_results} uses the $V_f=0.25$ design.

\subsection{Laminar-Design Mesh Independence}
\label{subsec:app_C_alexandersen_pipebend_lam_mesh}
Table~\ref{tab:app_C_alexandersen_pipebend_lam_mesh} reports the Ansys mesh-independence study for the extracted laminar pipe-bend design. This table is separate from the turbulent-design mesh study in Table~\ref{tab:app_B_alexandersen_pipebend_mesh} of Section~\ref{sec:app_B_config_alexandersen_pipebend} because the laminar and turbulent topologies generally produce different wall shapes and near-wall mesh requirements.
All Fluent runs were continued until the scaled residuals fell below $10^{-7}$ and the monitored integral quantities were stationary. Specifically, the static pressure drop was required to change by less than $5\times10^{-4}$ over $100$ iterations, while the viscous dissipation was required to change by less than $10^{-3}$ over $100$ iterations. \label{sec:app_C_alexandersen_ubend}

The U-bend tests whether the turbulent optimizer remains advantageous when the flow must complete a $180^\circ$ return path around a fixed separator. The turbulent case in Chapter~\ref{ch:benchmark_results} uses the $V_f=0.27$ design.

\label{subsec:app_C_alexandersen_ubend_topology}
Figure~\ref{fig:app_C_alexandersen_ubend_topologies} compares the laminar and turbulent optimized U-bend topologies. The expected distinction is that the laminar design may create an overly tight return path around the separator tip, while the turbulent design should form a smoother passage that reduces separation and turbulent mixing losses.
