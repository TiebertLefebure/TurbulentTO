\chapter{Laminar-Design Mesh Independence for Benchmark Topologies}
\label{app:C_laminar_turbulent_topology_comparison}

This appendix supports the laminar-design comparisons in Chapter~\ref{ch:benchmark_results}. It defines the common evaluation pipeline and reports the Ansys mesh-independence studies for the extracted laminar geometries. The topology figures and final laminar-versus-turbulent SST metric tables are placed in the corresponding Chapter~\ref{ch:benchmark_results} benchmark sections so that the main comparison can be read together with each optimization result.

\section{Methodology and Evaluation Pipeline}
\label{sec:app_C_lam_vs_turb_methodology}
Each benchmark is solved twice. The laminar reference design is optimized at $Re=1$, where the optimizer primarily responds to creeping-flow viscous losses. The turbulent design is optimized at the target high-Reynolds operating condition using the SA topology optimization framework described in Chapter~\ref{ch:topology_optimization_methodology}. Apart from replacing the SA turbulent state equations by the laminar flow formulation and setting $Re=1$, the laminar topology-optimization runs use the same benchmark-specific FEniCS setup as the turbulent runs: identical mesh, design-domain restrictions, non-design regions, volume constraint, prescribed inlet and outlet treatment, objective type, Brinkman penalty limits, filter radius, projection threshold, continuation schedules, MMA move limits, and maximum MMA iterations. Thus, the pipe-bend and U-bend comparisons retain the inlet-pressure objective $J_p$, while the diffuser comparison retains the dissipation objective $J_D$ from Yoon~\cite{Yoon2016}. This keeps the laminar-vs-turbulent comparison focused on the flow-physics model rather than on optimization or boundary-condition choices.

Because these FEniCS physical and numerical settings are inherited directly from the turbulent benchmark definitions in Appendix~\ref{app:B_validation_configurations}, they are not repeated as separate configuration tables in this appendix. The additional tables reported here are therefore limited to the Ansys mesh-independence studies for the extracted laminar geometries.

After optimization, both projected density fields are thresholded and extracted as sharp geometries. The extracted laminar and turbulent designs are then evaluated in Ansys Fluent under the same turbulent operating condition. Because Chapter~\ref{ch:benchmark_results} already validates the extracted turbulent topologies with both Ansys-SA and Ansys-SST, the laminar-design comparison uses the higher-fidelity SST $k$-$\omega$ closure. This keeps the comparison focused on topology performance under an independent two-equation turbulence model, rather than repeating the SA cross-code validation for the laminar reference geometries. The key performance metrics are viscous dissipation and pressure drop.

For fairness, the extracted laminar geometries receive their own Ansys mesh-independence checks. The turbulent-design grid studies are reported in Table~\ref{tab:app_B_alexandersen_pipebend_mesh} of Section~\ref{sec:app_B_config_alexandersen_pipebend}, Table~\ref{tab:app_B_alexandersen_ubend_mesh} of Section~\ref{sec:app_B_config_alexandersen_ubend}, and Table~\ref{tab:app_B_yoon_diffuser_mesh} of Section~\ref{sec:app_B_config_yoon_diffuser} in Appendix~\ref{app:B_validation_configurations}; this appendix adds the corresponding laminar-design mesh tables.

\section{Pipe Bend Benchmark}
\label{sec:app_C_alexandersen_pipebend}

The pipe bend tests whether a topology optimized under turbulent conditions produces a more separation-aware $90^\circ$ turning path than a laminar topology optimized at $Re=1$. The turbulent case in Chapter~\ref{ch:benchmark_results} uses the $V_f=0.25$ design.
The topology-level comparison and final SST performance table are reported in Section~\ref{subsec:alexandersen_pipebend_laminar}.

\subsection{Laminar-Design Mesh Independence}
\label{subsec:app_C_alexandersen_pipebend_lam_mesh}
Table~\ref{tab:app_C_alexandersen_pipebend_lam_mesh} reports the Ansys mesh-independence study for the extracted laminar pipe-bend design. This table is separate from the turbulent-design mesh study in Table~\ref{tab:app_B_alexandersen_pipebend_mesh} of Section~\ref{sec:app_B_config_alexandersen_pipebend} because the laminar and turbulent topologies generally produce different wall shapes and near-wall mesh requirements.
All Fluent runs were continued until the scaled residuals fell below $10^{-7}$ and the monitored integral quantities were stationary. Specifically, the static pressure drop was required to change by less than $5\times10^{-4}$ over $100$ iterations, while the viscous dissipation was required to change by less than $10^{-3}$ over $100$ iterations. Mass conservation was additionally checked from the inlet and outlet mass-flow reports.

\section{U-Bend Benchmark}
\label{sec:app_C_alexandersen_ubend}

The U-bend tests whether the turbulent optimizer remains advantageous when the flow must complete a $180^\circ$ return path around a fixed separator. The turbulent case in Chapter~\ref{ch:benchmark_results} uses the $V_f=0.27$ design.
The topology-level comparison and final SST performance table are reported in Section~\ref{subsec:alexandersen_ubend_laminar}.

\subsection{Laminar-Design Mesh Independence}
\label{subsec:app_C_alexandersen_ubend_lam_mesh}
Table~\ref{tab:app_C_alexandersen_ubend_lam_mesh} reports the Ansys mesh-independence study for the extracted laminar U-bend design. The turbulent U-bend mesh study is reported separately in Table~\ref{tab:app_B_alexandersen_ubend_mesh} of Section~\ref{sec:app_B_config_alexandersen_ubend}.
For the $Re=5{,}000$ U-bend operating point, the inlet-port height is $0.2L$ and $U_{\mathrm{in}}=1~\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, giving $\nu=4.0\times10^{-5}~\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$. Using the same smooth-wall estimate as the FEniCS mesh generator gives $u_\tau\approx6.59\times10^{-2}~\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and a wall distance of $y_{+}=1$ at $6.07\times10^{-4}~\mathrm{m}$. The Ansys mesh hierarchy therefore keeps the first layer fixed at $6.00\times10^{-4}~\mathrm{m}$ and refines the bulk triangular mesh, curvature resolution, and inflation-layer distribution around the separator tip and $180^\circ$ return wall.
All Fluent runs were continued until the scaled residuals fell below $10^{-7}$ and the monitored integral quantities were stationary. The static pressure drop was required to change by less than $5\times10^{-4}$ over $100$ iterations, while the viscous dissipation was required to change by less than $10^{-3}$ over $100$ iterations. Mass conservation was additionally checked from the inlet and outlet mass-flow reports.
