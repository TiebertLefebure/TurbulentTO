\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:ch5_introduction}
Building upon the mathematical and numerical framework developed in Chapter~\ref{ch:topology_optimization_methodology}, this chapter evaluates the turbulent topology optimization solver on established internal-flow benchmarks. The main emphasis is the Alexandersen pipe bend, because this is the working configuration used for the final thesis campaign: \texttt{reciprocal\_penalized} wall distance, \texttt{design} wall-density source, power-law SA and wall-distance penalties, and the frozen-turbulence adjoint. The chapter therefore first verifies the sensitivity calculation for this exact Alexandersen setup before discussing the topology optimization result.

The evaluation addresses three questions. First, does the implemented frozen adjoint produce derivatives that agree with central finite differences for the Alexandersen pipe bend? Second, does the resulting optimized topology reproduce the expected smooth, separation-avoiding bend behavior reported in the turbulent topology optimization literature? Third, does the extracted diffuse design remain credible when re-evaluated as a sharp geometry in Ansys Fluent with SA and SST $k$-$\omega$ turbulence models?

The Dilgen and Yoon cases are retained as supporting benchmarks. They document how the same solver family behaves on other turbulent topology optimization examples, but they are not used as the primary sensitivity verification for the final Alexandersen pipe-bend results. To maintain focus on the core turbulent physics and topology results, detailed geometric parameters, solver settings, and Ansys grid-independence studies are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:B_validation_configurations}. Similarly, the detailed laminar-versus-turbulent topology comparisons are consolidated in Appendix~\ref{app:C_laminar_turbulent_topology_comparison}.

\subsection{Optimization and Validation Metrics}
\label{subsec:ch5_metrics}
Throughout the optimization process, the objective $J$ follows the case-specific metric defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:topology_optimization_methodology}. Dissipation-driven cases use the Brinkman-penalized power dissipation in Eq.~\ref{eq:objective_function}, while the Alexandersen pipe bend uses the average inlet-pressure objective in Eq.~\ref{eq:inlet_pressure_objective}. The dissipation formulation smoothly combines physical viscous dissipation with the artificial resistance necessary to enforce solid regions. However, this diffuse metric cannot be directly compared to the results from Ansys Fluent. Once the geometry is extracted, the Brinkman penalty is removed, leaving discrete, sharp solid walls.

To bridge this gap, the primary validation metric is the physical viscous dissipation integrated over the active fluid domain,
\begin{equation}
	\Phi_D = \int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{design}}}
	\frac{1}{2} \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}
	\left(\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla\mathbf{u}^{T}\right):
	\left(\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla\mathbf{u}^{T}\right)
	\,d\Omega ,
	\label{eq:ch5_validation_dissipation}
\end{equation}
where the effective viscosity is $\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mu+\mu_t$ for turbulent evaluations and $\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mu$ for laminar reference solves. Furthermore, because pressure drop is the most direct and practical engineering interpretation of these flow losses, $\Delta p$ is also reported from the Ansys validation runs. All two-dimensional dissipation quantities are evaluated per unit depth.

\subsection{Wall-Distance and SA Penalization Conventions}
\label{subsec:ch5_wall_distance_note}
The final Alexandersen computations use one wall-distance and penalty convention throughout: \texttt{reciprocal\_penalized} wall distance, \texttt{design} wall-density source, and the power-law solid indicator in Eq.~\ref{eq:power_penalty_indicator}. The wall-distance field is updated in the forward loop, but it is treated as a frozen coefficient during the adjoint sensitivity solve. Thus, the finite-difference verification below checks the implemented frozen derivative for the same practical assumption used by the optimizer.

\section{Dilgen Pipe Bend: Supporting Sensitivity Diagnostic}
\label{sec:dilgen_pipebend}
The Dilgen pipe bend~\cite{Dilgen2018} is retained as a supporting diagnostic because it was used during development to compare frozen-adjoint sensitivities against central finite differences on a known turbulent bend. It is not the primary sensitivity verification for the final thesis campaign. The primary check is the Alexandersen pipe bend in Section~\ref{subsec:alexandersen_pipebend_sensitivity}, because that case uses the same \texttt{reciprocal\_penalized}, power-law, frozen-turbulence configuration as the reported optimization.

\subsection{Geometry and Boundary Conditions}
\label{subsec:dilgen_pipebend_setup}
The geometric domain and boundary conditions are configured according to the pipe-bend setup illustrated in Fig.~2 of Dilgen et al.~\cite{Dilgen2018}. While the original authors compared multiple turbulence models, the present implementation restricts its scope exclusively to the SA model. In Dilgen et al., the channel-bend verification is initialized with $\gamma=1$ throughout the design domain, and the SA distance function is not penalized for that verification. The present \texttt{passive} wall-density source and \texttt{poisson} wall-distance mode reproduce that choice.

\subsection{Sensitivity Verification Results}
\label{subsec:dilgen_pipebend_sensitivity}
To ensure the baseline flow physics are captured correctly, the forward FEniCS-SA velocity-magnitude contours are first computed (Fig.~\ref{fig:dilgen_pipebend_velocity}). The main high-speed stream follows the curved passage from inlet to outlet, while the inner recirculating part of the bend remains a low-velocity region. This reproduces the qualitative structure of the SA result reported by Dilgen et al.~\cite{Dilgen2018} and provides the necessary forward-state basis for interpreting the adjoint field.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{Dilgen-PipeBend-SA-Frozen-Velocity}
	\caption{Velocity-magnitude contours for the Dilgen pipe bend computed with the FEniCS-SA frozen-turbulence implementation, following the contour presentation of Dilgen et al.~\cite{Dilgen2018}.}
	\label{fig:dilgen_pipebend_velocity}
\end{figure}

With the flow field established, the spatial sensitivity field $dJ/d\gamma$ is evaluated. The coordinate-wise finite-difference verification follows the central-difference metric used by Dilgen et al.~\cite{Dilgen2018}. For a sampled DG0 design cell $i$ with unit perturbation vector $\mathbf{e}_i$, the frozen-turbulence finite-difference sensitivity used in the present table is
\begin{equation}
	S_i^{\mathrm{FD}}
	=
	\frac{
			J\!\left(\boldsymbol{\gamma}+\Delta h\,\mathbf{e}_i;
			\mathbf{u}^{+},p^{+},\tilde{\nu}^{0}\right)
		-
			J\!\left(\boldsymbol{\gamma}-\Delta h\,\mathbf{e}_i;
			\mathbf{u}^{-},p^{-},\tilde{\nu}^{0}\right)
	}{
		2\Delta h
	},
	\qquad \Delta h=10^{-6}.
	\label{eq:dilgen_pipebend_fd_metric}
\end{equation}
Here $J$ is the power-dissipation objective, corresponding to Dilgen's notation $C$, the superscripts $+$ and $-$ denote the converged flow states for the positively and negatively perturbed designs, and $\tilde{\nu}^{0}$ is the unperturbed SA working-variable field held fixed in the frozen-turbulence comparison. The discrepancy reported in Table~\ref{tab:dilgen_pipebend_fd_adjoint_comparison} is computed as
\begin{equation}
	\varepsilon_i^{(m)}
	=
	\frac{\left|S_i^{(m)}-S_i^{\mathrm{FD}}\right|}
	{\max\!\left(\left|S_i^{(m)}\right|,\left|S_i^{\mathrm{FD}}\right|,10^{-30}\right)},
	\label{eq:dilgen_pipebend_fd_relative_error}
\end{equation}
where $m$ denotes the adjoint mode being compared. The present table reports only the frozen-turbulence mode. For this fixed Poisson wall-distance case, the wall distance is passive with respect to the design field; there is no penalized wall-distance derivative, no reciprocal wall-distance state, and no $G$-adjoint contribution.

\section{Alexandersen Pipe Bend}
\label{sec:alexandersen_pipebend}
The Alexandersen pipe bend benchmark, originally formulated by Bayat, Li, and Alexandersen~\cite{Bayat2026}, introduces a critical cross-model verification. While the reference study employs a standard $k$-$\varepsilon$ RANS model with wall functions, the present framework tackles the exact same problem using a wall-resolved SA model under the frozen-turbulence assumption. This comparison provides valuable insight into whether the fundamental topological solution, a streamlined and separation-avoiding bend, remains consistent across fundamentally different turbulence closures.

\subsection{Geometry and Boundary Conditions}
\label{subsec:alexandersen_pipebend_setup}
The boundary conditions and geometric constraints replicate the setup shown in Fig.~10 of Bayat et al.~\cite{Bayat2026}, specifically targeting the $V_f=0.25$ volume-fraction case. In contrast to the dissipation-driven cases, the Alexandersen optimization minimizes the average inlet pressure while holding the outlet pressure fixed, so the objective directly represents the pressure loss required to drive the imposed flow through the evolving bend.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{Alexandersen-PipeBend-GeometryBCs}
	\caption{Geometry and boundary conditions for the Alexandersen pipe-bend benchmark, based on Bayat et al.~\cite{Bayat2026}. The present evaluation targets the $V_f=0.25$ case using the SA model.}
	\label{fig:alexandersen_pipebend_setup}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Sensitivity Verification}
\label{subsec:alexandersen_pipebend_sensitivity}
Before interpreting the optimized topology, the frozen adjoint is verified directly on the Alexandersen pipe-bend configuration. This is the relevant check for the final thesis setup because it uses the same power-law SA penalty, \texttt{reciprocal\_penalized} wall distance, \texttt{design} wall-density source, inlet-pressure objective, and frozen-turbulence adjoint used during the optimization.

Five active DG0 design cells are sampled deterministically from a random seed. For each sampled cell $i$, the raw density is perturbed by a central finite-difference step $\Delta h=10^{-6}$ while the SA working-variable field and reciprocal wall-distance field are held fixed. The perturbed velocity-pressure state is then re-solved, and the finite-difference derivative is computed as
\begin{equation}
	S_i^{\mathrm{FD}}
	=
	\frac{
			J_p\!\left(\boldsymbol{\gamma}+\Delta h\,\mathbf{e}_i;
			\mathbf{u}^{+},p^{+},\tilde{\nu}^{0},G^{0}\right)
		-
			J_p\!\left(\boldsymbol{\gamma}-\Delta h\,\mathbf{e}_i;
			\mathbf{u}^{-},p^{-},\tilde{\nu}^{0},G^{0}\right)
	}{
		2\Delta h
	}.
	\label{eq:alexandersen_pipebend_fd_metric}
\end{equation}
This verifies the derivative actually supplied to MMA: the flow adjoint accounts for the dependence of the inlet-pressure objective on the flow solution and the density penalties, while the turbulence and wall-distance updates are frozen during differentiation. The absolute and relative errors are
\begin{equation}
	\Delta_i^{\mathrm{abs}} =
	\left|S_i^{\mathrm{fr}}-S_i^{\mathrm{FD}}\right|,
	\qquad
	\varepsilon_i^{\mathrm{fr}}
	=
	\frac{\Delta_i^{\mathrm{abs}}}
	{\max\!\left(\left|S_i^{\mathrm{fr}}\right|,\left|S_i^{\mathrm{FD}}\right|,10^{-30}\right)} .
	\label{eq:alexandersen_pipebend_fd_relative_error}
\end{equation}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:ch5_conclusion}
This chapter centers the final thesis evidence on the Alexandersen pipe bend. The sensitivity verification is performed on the same \texttt{reciprocal\_penalized}, power-law, frozen-turbulence configuration used for the optimization, so the finite-difference comparison directly checks the derivative supplied to MMA. The resulting topology is then evaluated as the primary turbulent design case, with the Dilgen and Yoon benchmarks retained as supporting context for curved manifolds and adverse-pressure-gradient flows.

The independent CFD validation using Ansys Fluent is therefore interpreted as a sharp-geometry check of the Alexandersen design, not merely as another internal FEniCS result. Testing the extracted topology with both SA and SST $k$-$\omega$ quantifies how much of the optimized performance survives a change in turbulence closure. Together with the laminar comparisons detailed in the appendices, this chapter supports the thesis argument that high-Reynolds turbulence physics must be included directly in the optimization loop when designing viable internal-flow topologies.
