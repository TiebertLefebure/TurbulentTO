\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:ch5_introduction}
Building upon the mathematical and numerical framework developed in Chapter~\ref{ch:topology_optimization_methodology}, this chapter evaluates the performance of the turbulent topology optimization solver against established literature benchmarks. Unlike previous chapters that relied on scaling up laminar configurations, the cases presented here are fundamentally turbulent. By replicating the geometries, physical parameters, and simulation conditions of true turbulent benchmark papers, this chapter systematically addresses three core questions. First, do the implemented adjoint sensitivities behave consistently and predictably on a known turbulent flow problem? Second, do the resulting optimized topologies successfully capture the qualitative flow-routing and separation-avoidance behaviors reported in the wider turbulent topology optimization literature? Finally, do the extracted diffuse designs remain physically credible when evaluated as sharp, discrete geometries in an independent, industry-standard CFD solver (Ansys Fluent) using both the Spalart-Allmaras (SA) and SST $k$-$\omega$ turbulence models?

The selected cases are presented in an order of increasing complexity and interpretive depth. The evaluation begins with the Dilgen pipe bend, which serves strictly as a sensitivity verification case. It isolates the critical difference between a frozen-turbulence adjoint, governed by the state $(\mathbf{u}^*,p^*)$, and the fully coupled SA adjoint, which incorporates the turbulent working variable $(\mathbf{u}^*,p^*,\tilde{\nu}^*)$. Consistent with the setup inspired by Dilgen et al., the geometric wall-distance variable is omitted from this coupled adjoint state. 

Following this verification, the Dilgen U-bend is introduced as the first true topology optimization case, applying the same penalization and wall-distance conventions to generate an evolving design. The Alexandersen pipe bend is then evaluated to assess the solver's performance against an independent reference formulation based on a different turbulence closure. Ultimately, the Yoon diffuser benchmark is investigated to compare the framework's handling of adverse pressure gradients against the original SA-based topology optimization literature.

For the three true topology optimization cases, the discussion follows a consistent structure: a review of the geometry and boundary conditions, an analysis of the FEniCS optimization results, an independent CFD validation assessing turbulence-model sensitivity, and a comparative analysis against the equivalent laminar design. To maintain focus on the core turbulent physics and topology results, detailed geometric parameters, solver settings, and Ansys grid-independence studies are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:B_validation_configurations}. Similarly, the detailed laminar-versus-turbulent topology comparisons are consolidated in Appendix~\ref{app:C_laminar_turbulent_topology_comparison}.

\subsection{Optimization and Validation Metrics}
\label{subsec:ch5_metrics}
Throughout the optimization process, the objective $J$ is driven by the Brinkman-penalized power dissipation, as defined in Eq.~\ref{eq:objective_function}. This formulation is mathematically essential for gradient-based topology optimization, as it smoothly combines physical viscous dissipation with the artificial resistance necessary to enforce solid regions. However, this diffuse metric cannot be directly compared to the results from Ansys Fluent. Once the geometry is extracted, the Brinkman penalty is removed, leaving discrete, sharp solid walls.

To bridge this gap, the primary validation metric is the physical viscous dissipation integrated over the active fluid domain,
\begin{equation}
	\Phi_D = \int_{\Omega_{\mathrm{design}}}
	\frac{1}{2} \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}
	\left(\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla\mathbf{u}^{T}\right):
	\left(\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla\mathbf{u}^{T}\right)
	\,d\Omega ,
	\label{eq:ch5_validation_dissipation}
\end{equation}
where the effective viscosity is $\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mu+\mu_t$ for turbulent evaluations and $\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mu$ for laminar reference solves. Furthermore, because pressure drop is the most direct and practical engineering interpretation of these flow losses, $\Delta p$ is also reported from the Ansys validation runs. All two-dimensional dissipation quantities are evaluated per unit depth.

\subsection{Wall-Distance and SA Penalization Conventions}
\label{subsec:ch5_wall_distance_note}
It is important to note that the wall-distance construction varies slightly across the benchmark suite to align with the respective reference literature. The Dilgen pipe bend and U-bend employ the \texttt{poisson\_penalized} wall-distance mode. For the U-bend specifically, the SA wall source is dynamically tied to the evolving design field, accurately reflecting the creation of new turbulent walls during optimization. Conversely, the Yoon diffuser utilizes the reciprocal wall-distance logic inherent to Yoon's original SA formulation. The specific settings for the Alexandersen pipe bend are detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:B_validation_configurations}. 

\section{Dilgen Pipe Bend: Sensitivity Verification}
\label{sec:dilgen_pipebend}
The Dilgen pipe bend~\cite{Dilgen2018} serves as the foundational sensitivity verification case. Rather than acting as a full topology optimization run, this static geometry is used to confirm that the implemented SA sensitivities accurately reflect the complex flow physics of a turbulent bend. Crucially, it demonstrates how the inclusion of the coupled SA adjoint alters the design sensitivity field, particularly in the high-shear and flow-separation regions near the bend radius.

\subsection{Geometry and Boundary Conditions}
\label{subsec:dilgen_pipebend_setup}
The geometric domain and boundary conditions are configured according to the pipe-bend setup illustrated in Fig.~2 of Dilgen et al.~\cite{Dilgen2018}. While the original authors compared multiple turbulence models, the present implementation restricts its scope exclusively to the SA model. This targeted approach isolates the verification of the SA sensitivity machinery that underpins the subsequent optimizations in this chapter.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:ch5_conclusion}
Through a progressive sequence of established benchmarks, this chapter has demonstrated the capability and physical fidelity of the developed turbulent topology optimization framework. Beginning with the Dilgen pipe bend, the analysis confirmed that the coupled SA sensitivities appropriately dictate material changes in regions critical to turbulence production. The framework then successfully replicated the core topological strategies of the Dilgen U-bend and Alexandersen pipe bend, favoring sweeping turns that actively avoid momentum losses and corner separation, even when compared against alternate turbulence closures like $k$-$\varepsilon$. Finally, the Yoon diffuser benchmark underscored the framework's ability to manage complex adverse pressure gradients, rejecting abrupt expansions in favor of aerodynamically sound, gradual divergence.

Critically, the independent CFD validation using Ansys Fluent establishes that the advantages of turbulent optimization persist even when the diffuse mathematical domain is transformed into a sharp, manufacturable geometry. By testing these extracted topologies against the SST $k$-$\omega$ model, the results confirm that the generated layouts are fundamentally robust fluidic designs, rather than mere mathematical artifacts of the FEniCS-SA solver. Together with the laminar comparisons detailed in the appendices, this chapter solidifies the thesis argument: incorporating high-Reynolds turbulence physics directly into the optimization loop is not merely an incremental improvement, but an absolute necessity for generating viable engineering topologies.
